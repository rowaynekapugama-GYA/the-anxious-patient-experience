# Using AI to draft treatment plans: the options

For Dr Lorna Gladwin and GYA to decide on. Written 27 November 2026. This is not legal advice, and nothing here says
any option is compliant.

## What you asked

You asked whether AI could fill in plans the way you want, instead of typing each one by hand. Underneath that is a
simpler question: how do you stop retyping the same things for every patient? The website now does most of this
without AI:

- **Templates.** For example, "first visit with inhalation sedation".
- **A library of your standard paragraphs.**
- **Details filled in from the booking.** The patient's name, the appointment date and the dentist are added
  automatically.
- **Starting from an earlier plan.** Anything typed for that patient is cleared.

## Option 1: better templates (no patient data involved)

You tell us once, in your own words, how you want plans to read for the situations you see most. We turn that into
templates and library paragraphs for you. From then on you pick a template, adjust what is different for that patient,
and finalise.

No patient information goes anywhere new, and nothing changes in the privacy policy. This may be all you need.

## Option 2: AI drafting (patient information sent to an AI provider)

**What it would look like.** On a plan you type a few words about the patient, for example "anxious about needles,
two fillings lower left, wants short visits". The AI drafts the plan in your wording. You then read, edit and approve
it. Nothing is saved or sent without you, and it never runs on its own.

**What it means for privacy.** To draft a plan, the AI provider would receive health information about the patient.
Under the Privacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs), health information is "sensitive information",
which comes with stricter rules. The questions you would need answered:

- **Use and disclosure (APP 6):** is sending a patient's clinical notes to an AI provider within what the patient
  would reasonably expect, or does it need their consent?
- **Overseas recipients (APP 8):** most AI providers process data outside Australia. If yours does, you stay
  accountable for how it handles the information. Can you get processing in Australia, or a contract that holds the
  provider to the APPs?
- **Telling patients (APP 1 and APP 5):** the privacy policy would need a section on it, naming any overseas countries
  involved. Patients would probably need to be told at the time, for example in the booking notice. Would some
  patients want to opt out, and how would that work?
- **Security (APP 11):** what the provider keeps, for how long, and who at the provider can see it.
- **State law and professional guidance:** NSW has its own health privacy law (the Health Records and Information
  Privacy Act) with its own rules on sending health information interstate or overseas. The OAIC and AHPRA have both
  published guidance on using AI in health care, and AHPRA's guidance says the practitioner stays responsible for what
  goes to the patient.

It is worth a short conversation with your own adviser, or your indemnity insurer, before going ahead.

**What we would require if you choose it:**

- a provider that does not train on your data;
- a written agreement with the provider;
- nothing kept by the provider beyond the request;
- processing in Australia if available;
- every draft read and approved by you before it is saved;
- the same AHPRA wording check on the AI's output as on a typed plan;
- the whole feature behind a switch that is off by default, with every use in the audit log;
- the privacy policy updated before it is switched on.

## Recommendation

Start with the templates and paragraph library in this release and use them for a few weeks. If there is still a lot
of typing after that, we can look at AI drafting together, knowing exactly which part it would save and what the
privacy work would involve.
